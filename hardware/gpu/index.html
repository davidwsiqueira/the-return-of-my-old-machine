    GPU¶

    Specifications¶

    Component Details
    Manufacturer NVIDIA
    Model GeForce GT 430
    Architecture Fermi (GF108)
    Release Year 2010
    VRAM 1GB DDR3
    Memory Bus 128-bit
    DirectX Support DirectX 11
    OpenGL Support OpenGL 4.6
    PCI Express PCIe 2.0 x16
    TDP ~49W
    Cooling Single-fan air cooling
    Power Connector None required
    Linux Driver Support NVIDIA Proprietary Legacy Drivers

    The NVIDIA GeForce GT 430 is one of the most nostalgic parts of this restoration project.

    While it was originally considered an entry-level GPU, it represented a major step forward compared to integrated graphics of its era and was more than enough for lightweight gaming, multimedia acceleration, and general desktop usage.

    For this machine, the GPU carries emotional value as much as technical value.

    It was part of the system that introduced me to PC gaming and helped shape my interest in technology and software development.

    Even today, despite being heavily outdated for modern gaming standards, the GT 430 still provides:

    • Hardware video decoding
    • Multi-monitor support
    • Linux desktop acceleration
    • Retro gaming capability
    • Legacy DirectX/OpenGL compatibility

    Features¶

    VRAM¶

    The card includes:

    • 1GB DDR3 VRAM
    • 128-bit memory interface

    At launch, this was acceptable for: - 720p gaming - Older DirectX 9/10 titles - Multimedia workloads - Entry-level CUDA acceleration

    Today, VRAM capacity is one of the largest limitations of the card.

    Modern games frequently require: - 4GB minimum - 6GB–8GB recommended

    However, for Linux desktop usage and retro titles, 1GB remains usable.

    Outputs¶

    Typical GT 430 variants include combinations of:

    • VGA
    • DVI
    • HDMI

    This generation was important because HDMI support was becoming mainstream even on lower-end GPUs.

    That made the card useful not only for gaming, but also: - Home theater PCs - Multimedia systems - Secondary desktop machines

    Power Requirements¶

    One surprisingly good characteristic of the GT 430 is its low power consumption.

    Specification Value
    Approximate TDP 49W
    External PCIe Power Not required
    Recommended PSU 300W+

    The GPU draws power directly from the PCIe slot, which makes it ideal for older OEM systems and aging power supplies.

    For this restoration project, lower power consumption is actually beneficial because it reduces: - Heat generation - VRM stress - PSU load - Noise

    Cooling¶

    The card uses a simple single-fan cooling solution.

    During restoration, the GPU received:

    • Full dust cleaning
    • Fan disassembly
    • Fan washing using water and detergent
    • Internal inspection
    • Thermal paste replacement

    The fan cleaning process was especially satisfying because years of accumulated dust and dirt had significantly degraded the appearance of the cooler.

    After cleaning, the fan visually looked almost new again.

    Replacing the thermal paste was also important due to the age of the hardware.

    Old thermal compound tends to: - Dry out - Lose conductivity - Increase operating temperatures

    The maintenance restored: - Better thermal transfer - Lower temperatures - Quieter operation - Improved long-term reliability

    Performance¶

    By modern standards, the GT 430 is extremely limited.

    However, expectations matter.

    For this project, the goal is not modern AAA gaming.

    The GPU remains surprisingly usable for:

    Suitable Workloads¶

    • Linux desktop acceleration
    • Retro gaming
    • Emulation
    • Older PC games
    • Video playback
    • Development environments
    • Lightweight indie games

    Games Potentially Playable¶

    Examples of older or lightweight titles that may still run reasonably:

    • Half-Life 2
    • Left 4 Dead 2
    • Portal
    • Diablo III
    • Skyrim (low settings)
    • League of Legends
    • Minecraft
    • Older MMOs
    • Emulators

    The biggest bottlenecks today are: - VRAM capacity - Fermi architecture age - Driver support limitations - Raw shader performance

    Still, seeing a 2010 entry-level GPU continue functioning under Linux in 2026 feels genuinely impressive.

    Driver Support¶

    Linux support for older NVIDIA GPUs has become increasingly complicated over time.

    The GT 430 belongs to NVIDIA's Fermi generation, which is considered legacy hardware.

    Current situation:

    Driver Type Status
    Nouveau (open-source) Functional but limited
    NVIDIA Proprietary Legacy Driver Recommended
    Modern NVIDIA drivers Unsupported

    Under Linux Mint, the card can still operate properly using legacy NVIDIA drivers.

    However, support may become increasingly difficult as: - New kernels release - Wayland adoption increases - NVIDIA removes older compatibility branches

    Despite this, basic Linux desktop usage remains stable.

    Limitations¶

    Main limitations of the GT 430 today:

    Limitation Impact
    Only 1GB VRAM Severe modern gaming limitation
    DDR3 memory Low bandwidth
    Fermi architecture Very outdated
    No modern AV1 support Video limitations
    Legacy driver dependency Long-term Linux concern
    Weak shader performance Modern games struggle heavily

    Even lightweight modern games may require: - Reduced resolution - Low settings - Performance compromises

    Still, for the philosophy of this project, the GPU is not about raw power anymore.

    It represents: - Hardware preservation - Nostalgia - Engineering appreciation - Extending the life of aging technology

    Restoration Notes¶

    The GPU restoration process included:

    • Complete disassembly
    • Dust removal
    • Cooler cleaning
    • Fan washing with water and detergent
    • Thermal paste replacement
    • Visual inspection for capacitor damage
    • Heatsink cleaning

    Despite its age, the card survived restoration extremely well.

    The fact that a GPU released in 2010 still functions correctly today after cleaning and maintenance says a lot about the durability of older hardware.

    Linux Compatibility

    Linux compatibility has been surprisingly good considering the age of the GPU.

    The card still provides stable desktop acceleration and multimedia support under Linux Mint XFCE.

    However, long-term concerns remain:

    • NVIDIA legacy driver deprecation
    • Wayland compatibility
    • Kernel support evolution
    • Increasing dependence on open-source Nouveau drivers

    For retro gaming and lightweight Linux usage, the GT 430 remains far more usable than most people would expect.
